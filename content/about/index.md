Dr. Jakob Johnson is a graduate researcher at Los Alamos National Laboratory whose work addresses challenging problems in image processing, computer vision, and scientific machine learning. He develops methods for extracting reliable, meaningful information from complex visual and multimodal data, with an emphasis on computational approaches that remain effective when observations are limited or practical constraints make analysis difficult.

He earned his Ph.D. in Computing from the University of Utah in 2026 under the mentorship of Dr. Tolga Tasdizen. He previously received an M.S. in Data Science from the University of Utah in 2022 and a B.S. in Mathematics and Statistics from Utah State University in 2019. Together, this training provided him with a broad foundation in mathematics, statistics, data analysis, and computational research, enabling him to connect theoretical methods with practical scientific applications.

His recent research focuses on data fusion and uncertainty quantification, particularly in low-data environments where limited observations can make robust inference difficult. His published work applies machine learning and data-fusion methods to nuclear-forensics problems, while his earlier projects explored image segmentation and interactive data visualization. Across these efforts, he is interested in combining mathematics, statistics, and computer science to build principled computational tools and increase confidence in data-driven results. Beyond his primary research, he maintains technical interests in Linux, homelab system administration, and computer security.

Outside of his professional work, he enjoys cycling and hiking during the summer and skiing in the winter.

## Projects
- [Semi-supervised Image Segmentation](https://github.com/jakobottar/gimondi) - My Masters project, focusing on improving the accuracy of a segmentation U-Net architecture utilizing unlabeled data. 
- [PL Season Explorer](https://jakobottar.github.io/pl-season-explorer/) - A class project, visualizing the 2020 Premier League season stats on the web using [D3.js](https://d3js.org/).
